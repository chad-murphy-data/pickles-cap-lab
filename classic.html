How the computer drafts

What does the live power rank mean?

It compares the 20 rosters as they stand after each pick, including mixed doubles and DreamBreakers. Each empty roster spot gets an anonymous profile with the same gender’s median doubles and singles ratings among doubles ranks 61–100. These placeholders are identical across teams; their two median ratings need not describe one real player.

It measures strength so far. It does not anticipate future picks, spending or roster availability, and the placeholder rosters need not meet the budget rules. When all rosters are complete, the same calculation produces the final modeled round-robin win rates.

Computer owners buy the most expensive available player in round one, then search for strong roster completions under the cap. They use the same doubles, mixed-doubles and DreamBreaker model as the builder. Your picks are entirely up to you.

This is a fast, limited search for a playable game. It uses a smaller search than the research drafts and does not guarantee optimal picks. Owners can’t see your future choices. Each pick must leave a verified way for every team to finish inside the budget; the fast feasibility check can occasionally exclude a pick that a more complex allocation would allow. All teams draft from the same top 100 doubles-rated players of each gender, obey the same roster rules, and have only one copy of each player available. No actual players or money are involved.
