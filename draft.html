    Rules and how the model works

    How the computer drafts

    How are the live rankings calculated?

    After each pick, the model compares the 20 teams, including mixed doubles and DreamBreakers. To compare unfinished teams fairly, it fills each empty spot with the same placeholder for that gender. The placeholder uses the median doubles and singles ratings of players ranked 61–100 in doubles. Those two ratings may not belong to any one player.

    The rankings describe the players picked so far. They don’t predict future picks or spending, and the placeholder teams may not fit the budget. Once all teams are complete, the same calculation gives the final standings.

    Each computer team starts with the highest-priced available player it can fit into a complete team. After that, it looks for strong combinations within its budget. It uses the same match model as the team builder. You can choose any available player who fits your budget and roster rules.

    All teams share one player pool: the top 100 doubles-rated women and men. Each player can join only one team. Every pick must leave a way for all teams to finish with three women, three men and a $500k–$1M total.

    The computer uses a quick search, so it may miss a better choice. Its budget check can also rule out a pick that a more thorough search could allow. It cannot see your future picks. These are fantasy teams and prices.
